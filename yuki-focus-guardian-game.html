How to play

1 Open Yuki Tap the Yuki app and begin the 30-second demonstration focus session.
2 Test the boundary Try opening distracting and productive apps to see how Yuki responds.
3 Build the relationship Complete or end sessions and notice how Yuki’s trust, language, and expressions change.

Why this game

Can a focus tool feel like a relationship instead of a restriction?

I wanted to make staying focused feel less clinical and a little more human. Many focus tools rely on timers, locks, and warnings. Yuki turns that boundary into a character relationship: a tiny yeti guards distracting apps, encourages productive choices, and remembers how you respond to the commitment you made.

From an HCI perspective, this explores emotional design, anthropomorphism, feedback, and commitment. Yuki’s expressions make system states immediately understandable, the escalating reactions reveal repeated attempts to break focus, and the relationship meter turns longer-term behavior into something visible.

I also wanted Yuki to feel alive without becoming noisy or distracting. The character uses small movements—breathing, blinking, a shifting scarf and tail, a boop reaction, and expressive pose changes—so the animation supports the interaction instead of competing with it.

The idea also raises an ethical question. Emotional attachment can make a commitment feel meaningful, but guilt can become coercive when a product makes users feel punished for changing their minds. A real focus tool should preserve user control, provide a clear exit, and use the relationship to encourage rather than shame.

This is a playful interaction and animation prototype, not a clinical productivity tool. The app names and visual references are illustrative; this project is not affiliated with the brands shown.
