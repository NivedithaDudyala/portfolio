Why this game

A digital doorway into an everyday art form

Muggu is a traditional threshold-drawing practice associated with Telugu-speaking regions of South India. Patterns are built from chukkalu—carefully placed dots—then shaped with looping lines, curves, and repeated forms. Traditionally drawn with rice flour, and now also with chalk or stone powder, each muggu transforms an ordinary entrance into a space of attention and welcome.

We chose this game because the interaction already lives inside the art: connect dots, repeat a line, place a motif, and find balance through symmetry. Rather than turning muggu into a score-based puzzle, the game focuses on the calm rhythm of making and the freedom to create a pattern in your own way.

Muggu is significant because it brings creativity into daily life. The designs are often redrawn, adapted, and passed between generations, carrying regional visual knowledge through an art form that is both temporary and continuously renewed.

This is not a simulation of every regional style or the full skill behind traditional muggu. It is a playful introduction designed to spark curiosity about the practice, its visual logic, and the cultural meaning carried by each pattern.
