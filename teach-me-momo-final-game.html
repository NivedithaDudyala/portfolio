How to play

1 Try something Choose one of the three controls. There is no tutorial to memorize.
2 Watch Momo Pay attention to Momo’s reaction and to the visual cues around the character.
3 Adjust Use what happened to guide your next choice and work out the hidden rule.

Why this game

What learning by interaction teaches us about onboarding

I came to HCI from neuroscience, and I wanted this game to show how people can learn a system through action, feedback, and repetition. We constantly form expectations about cause and effect: we try something, observe the outcome, and update what we think will happen next.

That process matters in HCI because an interface should not depend entirely on instructions. Clear reactions, consistent patterns, and visible consequences can help people understand what a product does while they are already using it.

I intentionally removed the tutorial from this game. Momo’s expressions, the changing visual cue, and the response to each choice become the teaching system. Early rounds offer more support; later rounds reduce it. Wrong choices are safe and even make the cue easier to notice, so failure provides information instead of punishment.

The HCI lesson is not that every product should hide its instructions. It is that onboarding becomes stronger when the interface reinforces the explanation: actions produce immediate feedback, patterns stay consistent, and mistakes help the user recover. The interface itself should participate in teaching.

This is a playful demonstration, not a scientific assessment of learning. It is one example of how I connect neuroscience and HCI; the other games explore different ideas from perception and cognition through interaction.
